Only 2009 survives as a complete table in the sources consulted. The league's own site carries standings for recent seasons; wiring them in is the first job once you have the API.

Getting live scores in here

Standings, box scores and news all come from one place: the JSON feed behind bsnpr.com. Open the site in Chrome, press F12, take the Network tab, filter to Fetch/XHR and reload. The request that populates the standings table is the one you want. Copy it as cURL, confirm it works outside the browser, then drop the response into the STANDINGS object in this file, keyed by year. The rendering already exists — it only needs data.

The mobile app's package name is io.genius.bsnpr, which means game data flows through Genius Sports in FIBA LiveStats format. If you find that endpoint you get per-game box scores, not just season totals.

La Cuadrícula

Nine cells. Name a player who satisfies both the club on the left and the condition on top. One guess per cell, no repeats across the board.

The daily board is generated from today's date, so every person opening this file anywhere gets the same nine cells. Practice mode shuffles freely and does not count toward the streak. Boards are only issued when all nine cells have a verified answer in the pool, so every grid is solvable.

Refuerzos

Every BSN roster is split between nativos and refuerzos — imported players signed from leagues abroad. How many imports a team may carry is the most politically charged rule in Puerto Rican basketball, and it changed in 2024.

What it did to the 2025 leaderboard

The clearest measure of the rule's effect. Every one of the top four scorers in 2025 was an import; the highest-placed local was fifth, and he plays as a nativizado.

RankPlayerClubStatusPointsPer game

Where locals held ground: Iván Gandía of Aguada led the league in three-pointers with 91 in 27 games at 44 per cent, and Alexander Kappos of Caguas was second with 78.

Where the 2025 imports came from

Eleven of the league's imports arrived from Australia's NBL, whose calendar frees up players in BSN season. Mayagüez filled all three of its slots from that one league.

A longer history than the rule suggests

Importing is not new. Raymond Dalmau first came to Puerto Rico in 1966, recruited to reinforce the Piratas de Quebradillas — at that time players of Puerto Rican descent born off the island counted as imports. He stayed twenty years, retired as the league's all-time leader in points, rebounds and assists, and now has a coliseum named after him. The category a player is filed under has never mapped neatly onto who the league belongs to.

Sources and gaps

A stats archive that hides its holes is worse than one that shows them. Everything here is single-source unless noted.

The unresolved conflict. For 1945, the English Wikipedia franchise table credits Capitalinos de San Juan; the Spanish Wikipedia champions list credits Santos de San Juan. Both agree Gallitos de la UPR finished second. These may be the same club under two names, or one source may simply be wrong. This archive carries the English version. It has not been verified.

Adding real logos and photos

Every crest and portrait in this archive is drawn in code, so nothing is ever broken or missing. Each one also sits under an image slot: put a file at the right path and it takes over automatically, with no code change. Make a folder called img next to this file, with crest and player inside it.

WhatWhere it goesExample
Club logoimg/crest/<key>.pngimg/crest/bay.png
Player photoimg/player/<slug>.jpgimg/player/raymond-dalmau.jpg

Franchise keys are the three-letter codes shown in the head-to-head matrix, lowercased — bay, sge, pon, que and so on. Player slugs are the name lowercased with accents and punctuation stripped and spaces turned into hyphens. Open the browser console and run slug("Mario «Quijote» Morales") if you are unsure of one. PNG, SVG, JPG and WebP all work for crests; JPG, PNG and WebP for portraits. Square-ish logos and roughly 3:4 portraits look best. Set USE_IMAGES to false near the top of the script to switch everything back to the drawn marks.

The lead worth chasing

Wikipedia's scoring-champion article cites its numbers to the retired bsnpr.com stats engine at estadisticas/lideres.asp?anio=YYYY, with citations running from anio=1957 to anio=2004, all retrieved in July 2021. The league published a season-leaders database going back to 1957 — fifty-four years deeper than RealGM. Those URLs now return 404; the site was rebuilt as a JavaScript app and the ASP pages left the live web. They should still be in the Wayback Machine. Enumerate them through the CDX API, fetch each snapshot with the id_ suffix for raw HTML, and parse the tables.
